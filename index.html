Following the threshold reset in April 2025, the permanent England and Northern Ireland rates remain in force across the 2026–2027 tax year. Stamp Duty works in slices—much like income tax—so you only pay each percentage on the part of the purchase price that sits inside that band.

Budgeting with a Stamp Duty Calculator UK First Time Buyer 2027 Guide

When people sit down with a stamp duty calculator uk first time buyer 2027 estimate before making an offer, the headline tax figure is only half the story. Three rules catch first-time buyers off guard far more often than the percentage rates themselves.

1. Watch the £500,000 cliff-edge when negotiating

On a £500,000 home, a first-time buyer pays £10,000 in SDLT and saves £5,000 compared with a home mover. Offer £501,000—just £1,000 more—and your tax bill jumps straight to £15,050. That extra £1,000 on the purchase price costs £5,050 in tax overnight. If you are shopping in London or the South East around the half-million mark, negotiating to keep the contract price at or below £500,000 makes an immediate difference to your completion statement.

2. Joint purchases require every buyer to be a first-time buyer

HMRC assesses First-Time Buyer Relief on the transaction as a whole, not per person. If you are buying with a partner who owned a flat years ago—or even inherited a share in a family home—neither of you can claim the relief. The purchase switches to Standard Home Mover rates. Switch the toggle above to "Standard Home Mover" to see the exact figure you will need in that situation.

3. Plan for the 14-day payment window after completion

Your SDLT return and payment must reach HMRC within 14 calendar days of completion. In practice, your conveyancing solicitor will ask you to transfer the Stamp Duty alongside your remaining deposit and legal fees a few working days before completion so they can file the return on your behalf. Keep these extra cash costs in your budget alongside SDLT:

  • Conveyancing and local searches: Typically £1,200 to £2,200 depending on whether the property is freehold or leasehold.
  • RICS property survey: Around £450 to £1,200 for a Level 2 HomeBuyer Report or Level 3 Building Survey.
  • Mortgage arrangement fee: Often £0 to £1,499; paying it upfront avoids paying interest on it over 25–35 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions UK first-time buyers ask most often before exchanging contracts.

How much stamp duty does a first-time buyer pay in 2026 and 2027?
Under the post-April 2025 rules applying through 2026 and 2027 in England and Northern Ireland, first-time buyers pay 0% Stamp Duty on the first £300,000 of a home costing £500,000 or less, and 5% on the portion between £300,001 and £500,000. For example, a £350,000 purchase results in £2,500 of SDLT.
What happens if my first home costs more than £500,000?
If the purchase price goes even £1 above £500,000, First-Time Buyer Relief disappears completely. You pay the standard residential SDLT rates from the first pound: 0% up to £125,000, 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, and 5% on the slice from £250,001 to £925,000. On a £505,000 property, that adds £5,250 compared with buying at £500,000.
Can we claim First-Time Buyer Relief if only one of us has never owned a property?
No. To qualify for First-Time Buyer Relief on a joint purchase, every buyer named on the mortgage and title deeds must be a genuine first-time buyer who has never owned a freehold or leasehold residential interest anywhere in the world—including an inherited share of a home.
When do I have to pay Stamp Duty, and can I add it to my mortgage?
HMRC requires your SDLT return and full payment within 14 calendar days of completion. In practice, your conveyancing solicitor collects the money from you just before completion and pays HMRC on your behalf. While a few lenders allow you to increase your borrowing to free up cash, most buyers pay SDLT from savings alongside their deposit and legal fees.
Does this stamp duty calculator uk first time buyer 2027 cover Scotland or Wales?
No. SDLT applies only to postcodes in England and Northern Ireland. Buyers in Scotland pay Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) with a £175,000 nil-rate band for first-time buyers, while buyers in Wales pay Land Transaction Tax (LTT), which uses a £225,000 zero-rate threshold with no separate first-time buyer relief.

Buying in Scotland or Wales?

Property transaction tax is devolved across the UK. Check which postcode your new home falls under before budgeting—England and Northern Ireland use HMRC's SDLT calculated above, whereas Scotland and Wales run separate systems.

Scotland: LBTT

Collected by Revenue Scotland. First-time buyers benefit from a higher nil-rate threshold of £175,000 (instead of £145,000), worth up to £600 in relief. Above £175,000, rates start at 2% up to £250,000 and 5% from £250,001 to £325,000.

Wales: LTT

Collected by the Welsh Revenue Authority. Wales does not offer a separate first-time buyer relief, because its standard main-residence nil-rate band already starts higher at £225,000. Above that, LTT charges 6% between £225,001 and £400,000, and 7.5% from £400,001 to £750,000.
